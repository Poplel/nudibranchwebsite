Nudibranch — FAQ

FAQ

Short answers. The Guide will have the long ones.

The basics

What is Nudibranch, exactly?

A self-hosted music library manager and player. It runs on your own server alongside Jellyfin, handles the fiddly parts of keeping a music library — finding, downloading, tagging, staging, importing, deduplicating — and gives you a web player and an app for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Do I need Jellyfin?

Yes. Jellyfin is the music server; Nudibranch is the layer around it. Playlists are stored in Jellyfin when it's configured, play history is mirrored to it, and imports end up in the folder Jellyfin scans.

Does it replace Jellyfin's own music apps?

It can. The Nudibranch app is a full player — background audio, lock-screen transport, crossfade, ReplayGain, an equalizer, offline downloads — but its headline feature is management: reviewing and approving what happens to your library, from your phone.

Is it free?

The server is open source and free, on GitHub. There is no subscription, no account with us, and nothing to pay for on the server side.

What does it run on?

Docker Compose — an API container, a background worker and a web frontend, with a SQLite database and a handful of file volumes. Anything that runs Docker will do: a NAS, an old laptop, a VPS.

The app

Where do I get the app?

It's on its way to the App Store. Until then, keep an eye on the repository — or get in touch if you want to test it.

Is there an Android version?

No. The app is SwiftUI, for iPhone, iPad and Mac. Everything the app does goes through the server's REST API, though, so any client is possible in principle — and the web player works in any browser on any platform.

Does playback work offline?

Browsing does, always — the library's metadata is cached on the device. Listening offline needs an explicit download of the tracks, albums or playlists you want to take with you.

What about CarPlay?

Not yet. It's a priority, but it needs Apple's CarPlay audio entitlement before it can ship.

Can I use Siri or Shortcuts?

Yes. The app exposes Shortcuts actions and responds to Siri requests, and it can act as a remote playback target — a Shortcut or an automation can tell a specific device to start playing something.

Privacy and notifications

What data do you collect?

None. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers, no third-party SDKs that phone home, and no account with us. The full detail is in the privacy policy.

Then how do push notifications work?

Apple requires developer credentials to send a push, which self-hosters shouldn't need to obtain. So the app registers its Apple push token with a small relay run by the developer; your server signs a notification, the relay hands it to Apple, Apple delivers it. The relay never sees your username, password, library or listening history.

If you'd rather it didn't exist: point the app at your own relay, or give your server its own Apple push credentials and skip the relay entirely.

Does my server need to be exposed to the internet?

Only if you want to use it away from home. Plenty of people run it on the LAN and reach it over a VPN or a tunnel. If you do expose it, put it behind HTTPS and change the default admin PIN and pre-seeded API key first.

Libraries and users

Where does the music come from?

Wherever you point it. Nudibranch imports files you already have, and — if you configure them — can fetch from sources like a slskd instance or yt-dlp. What you download, and whether you're entitled to it, is between you and the law where you live.

Will it reorganise my existing files?

Not without asking. Imports, metadata edits and maintenance tools stage their changes as proposals that you approve or reject. Deletions go to a trash folder and are kept for a configurable retention period before being purged.

Can other people use my server?

Yes — it's multi-user, with per-flow permissions and per-device sessions. You can give someone the ability to listen and request music without giving them the ability to approve downloads or run maintenance tools.

Can I automate things?

Yes. Automations are trigger → action → notify: fire on a schedule, an interval, a webhook, a Shortcut, or an event like a finished download or a matched wishlist item.
